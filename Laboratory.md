## 1. Overview and Methodology

This lab exercise investigates the functionality of the ESP32's internal peripherals, specifically focusing on the Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC), Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) capabilities, and the Digital-to-Analog Converter (DAC).

*Equipment Note (Regarding Example 5):* Due to the lack of a digital multimeter during the experiment, DAC voltage measurements in Example 5 were obtained using an **ADC Loopback approach**. The actual analog signal generated by the DAC (GPIO 25) was routed directly into an ADC pin (GPIO 34) via a jumper wire. This allowed the microcontroller to calculate the voltage internally, ensuring we measured genuine DAC voltage outputs rather than averaged digital PWM signals.

### Analysis of Predicted vs. Actual Findings

* **Theoretical Expectations:** A perfectly linear 12-bit ADC spanning a $0.00\text{ V}$ to $3.30\text{ V}$ range would ideally read $0\text{ mV}$ at code $0$, $1650\text{ mV}$ at the midpoint (code $2048$), and $3300\text{ mV}$ at the maximum limit (code $4095$).

* **Actual Findings:** The empirical data showed expected deviations caused by the ESP32's inherent hardware limitations:

  1. **Zero-level Offset:** At code $0$, the measured voltage was $142\text{ mV}$ instead of absolute zero, which is a result of the ADC's non-linear deadband near ground.

  2. **High-end Saturation:** At the maximum code of $4095$, the voltage maxed out at $3160\text{ mV}$ ($3.16\text{ V}$) instead of the theoretical $3.30\text{ V}$. With an $11\text{ dB}$ attenuation, the ESP32 ADC hits its ceiling around $3.16\text{ V}$, meaning any physical voltage above this threshold simply registers as $4095$.

## 3. Example 4: LED Brightness Control via PWM

### Objective

To translate the 12-bit potentiometer readings (0–4095) into an 8-bit PWM signal (0–255) to regulate the brightness of an LED connected to GPIO 19, utilizing the `ledcAttach()` function from the ESP32 Core v3.x.

### Analysis of Predicted vs. Actual Findings

* **Theoretical Expectations:** The corresponding 8-bit PWM value was calculated using this scaling formula:
  

  $$
  \text{Expected Duty} = \left\lfloor \frac{\text{Raw ADC Value} \times 255}{4095} \right\rfloor
  $$

* **Actual Findings:** The practical duty cycle generated matched the mathematical formula flawlessly (e.g., an ADC reading of $1834$ accurately resulted in a duty cycle of $114$). However, while the electrical duty cycle increased perfectly linearly, the human eye perceived the LED's brightness change non-linearly. The LED appeared to brighten much faster at the lower end of the duty cycle, which is a normal characteristic of human visual perception.

### Concept Question: Restricting Duty Cycle to 128

*If the maximum duty cycle is restricted to 128 without altering the frequency, what aspect of the waveform is modified?*

**Answer:** The base frequency ($f = 5\text{ kHz}$) and the overall signal period ($T = 200\text{ }\mu\text{s}$) stay exactly the same. What changes is the maximum **pulse width duration (**$T_{\text{HIGH}}$**)**. When the potentiometer is turned all the way up, the signal will only remain HIGH for half of the period ($100\text{ }\mu\text{s}$, or a 50% duty cycle). As a result, the maximum possible physical brightness of the LED is reduced by approximately half.

### Analysis of Predicted vs. Actual Findings

* **Theoretical Expectations:** A perfect 8-bit DAC operating on a $3.30\text{ V}$ reference should scale up linearly by $12.94\text{ mV}$ with each step, beginning at $0.00\text{ V}$ and topping out at $3.30\text{ V}$ at code $255$.

* **Actual Findings:**

  1. **Lower Bound (Code 0):** The output read $0.14\text{ V}$ (a $+140\text{ mV}$ discrepancy). This indicates the internal buffer driver cannot pull the voltage completely down to true ground.

  2. **Middle Values (Codes 64–192):** The measured voltages closely followed the ideal linear progression, showing very minor offsets (between $0.00\text{ V}$ and $-0.08\text{ V}$).

  3. **Upper Bound (Code 255):** The maximum voltage capped at $3.14\text{ V}$ (a $-0.16\text{ V}$ discrepancy). This demonstrates that the DAC's output amplifier hits its saturation limit roughly $160\text{ mV}$ below the actual $3.3\text{ V}$ power rail.

### Technical Distinction: PWM vs. True DAC

* **Pulse Width Modulation (PWM):** Creates a high-frequency digital signal that rapidly toggles between $0\text{ V}$ and $3.3\text{ V}$. It mimics an analog voltage strictly by altering the width of the HIGH pulses.

* **Digital-to-Analog Converter (DAC):** Relies on an internal R-2R resistor network and an output buffer to deliver a stable, continuous analog DC voltage, completely free of digital toggling.
